Notes to Parents and Students

Creativity is unleashed when we learn methods that we can then adapt. I have found this to be true whenever I have taught structures to students over the years. Students who are stuck and don’t know how to begin, how to continue, or how to end, are liberated by having a structure within which they can work. Once you have learned it well, you can adapt it any way you like. This is how all of the great authors learned to write - start with a structure that has been used before, then adapt to suit your own ideas.

We will be using various different structures, combined with images that stimulate ideas, to guide our creative writing. They are as follows:

Structure One: Shifting Focus

Paragraph One: Description of surroundings: wide focus

Paragraph Two: Description of surroundings: narrow focus

Paragraph Three: Description of character

Paragraph Four: Flashback

Paragraph Five: Jolt back to present

Paragraph Six: Conclusion (happy or tragic)

Structure Two: A Hero’s Journey

Paragraph One: Setting out

Paragraph Two: Meeting danger

Paragraph Three: Facing setbacks

Paragraph Four: Overcoming setbacks to achieve a goal

Paragraph Five: The journey home

Paragraph Six: The return - how the hero has changed / what they have learned

Structure Three: Start in the Middle

Paragraph One: In medias res (in the middle of things)

Paragraph Two: Flashback: how we got here

Paragraph Three: Jolt back to the present

Paragraph Four: Struggle with danger or difficulty

Paragraph Five: Resolution of danger or difficulty (happy or tragic)

Structure Four: Rising and Falling Action

Paragraph One: A hope for the future

Paragraph Two: A plan for success

Paragraph Three: A triumph

Paragraph Four: A setback

Paragraph Five: A struggle

Paragraph Six: Conclusion (happy or tragic)<br>

## 01. Stormy Sea

Stormy Sea (Structure One: Shifting Focus) 

| Paragraph | Vocabulary |
| --- | --- |
| Paragraph One: Description of surroundings: wide focus.<br>The huge waves towered over the ship. | roared, crashed, sides, terrifying, mountainous, lurched, swallowed, ocean, escaped |
| Paragraph Two: Description of surroundings: narrow focus.<br>The small lifeboat made its desperate way across the angry seas.<br><br>it’s =  it is | distant coastline, climbed, summit, plunged, down, threatened, struggle, survival, bailing out, staying afloat, drowning, capsized |
| Paragraph Three: Description of character.<br>Grimly determined, the sailor rowed on towards his destination. | exhausted, near despair, survive, hope, wife, children, return, safely, inspired, thought, oars |
| Paragraph Four: Flashback.<br>He remembered the day they had set out on this voyage. | sunshine, hope, joy, excited, adventure, calm sea, blue sky, optimistic, success |
| Paragraph Five: Jolt back to present.<br>A slap of salty water jolted him back to reality. | chilling, spray, bitter, breathless, shock, refocus, challenge, persevered |
| Paragraph Six: Conclusion: happy or tragic<br>Slowly but surely, the distant coastline came nearer. | safety, refuge, struggle, triumph, dry land, weeping, relief, welcomed, warm, ordeal, locals |

## 62. The Charge of the Light Brigade

The Charge of the Light Brigade (Structure Based on Tennyson’s Poem)

This famous charge happened during the Crimean War, a conflict in Eastern Europe between the Russians on one side, and Britain and her allies on the other. On 25th October 1854, following a misunderstanding about an order from high command, Lord Cardigan led the Light Brigade in a frontal assault on well armed and well positioned Russian artillery and infantry units. ‘Into Exile’

    She knew she had to leave. This home could be home no longer. The harsh sounds of the German soldiers’ speech rang in her ears as she struggled onwards over the rutted road, leading a horse and cart that contained all of her earthly possessions, and her two fatherless children. They travelled on wearily, out of the village that had been her home all of her life, and into the open countryside, seeking refuge from the endless conflict and the hated occupying soldiers.

    As the sun sank beneath the forests, the woman thought desperately about how she would shelter for the night. She had always slept between solid walls. The sounds of the forest filled her with terror as she thought of the wild creatures that lurked behind the darkening trunks of its trees.

    She realised that she did not even know how to build a fire. With the help of her sons, she gathered fallen branches and leaves from the side of the road, and piled them up in what looked like the right order. But how was she to ignite them?

    The answer came from an unexpected direction. The dark shape of a man loomed closer as he walked awkwardly towards her through the evening gloom of the forest. He was evidently suffering from an injury that made it difficult and painful to walk, but he persevered, and as he neared her, she began to think that he looked familiar. He sat down beside her and offered to light the fire. He took out a cigarette lighter and achieved this with ease. She kept asking herself where she had seen him before. She still had not answered the question when he rose, and despite her protests, walked on through the darkening forest, leaving her to warm herself by the blaze he had created.

    The next morning was damp and chill. The cold ashes of last night’s fire afforded no comfort. The woman shivered as she prepared to carry on her journey into exile. All at once, it dawned upon her who that man was whom she had only half-recognised the night before. Why had she not realised at the time? It was her own husband. But it could not have been! He had been killed at the front  two years before. And if it was he, why had he not stayed?

    Whatever questions she had about her husband’s mysterious visit, it clarified something in her mind. His appearance convinced her to return to her village. However painful it was, she had conceived a new determination to carry on and to rebuild the place that she loved. One day the war would be over. She had to persevere and hope for better times. She had to remain loyal to her birthplace. She trudged back along the bumpy road, an exile no longer.
